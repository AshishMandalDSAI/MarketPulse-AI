# MarketPulse AI — Marketing Metrics Glossary (MBA-friendly)

| Term | Plain-language meaning |
|---|---|
| **Naive ROAS** | Revenue ÷ Marketing Spend for a channel, with no adjustment for carryover or diminishing returns. Simple, but can overstate a channel's true impact if a lot of its "sales" would have happened anyway. |
| **Adstock (carryover)** | The idea that an ad's effect doesn't vanish the moment you stop spending — a TV campaign this week can still be selling product two or three weeks from now. |
| **Saturation (diminishing returns)** | Beyond a point, spending more on a channel earns smaller and smaller additional sales — the audience or ad inventory gets used up. |
| **Model-estimated contribution** | How much revenue the fitted model attributes to a channel, worked out by asking "what would revenue have been without this channel's spend?" It's a modeling estimate, not an audited fact. |
| **Model-estimated efficiency** | Estimated contribution ÷ spend for a channel — "how many rupees of estimated revenue do we get per rupee spent," according to the model. |
| **R² (R-squared)** | The share of week-to-week revenue variation the model explains, from 0% (no better than guessing the average) to 100% (perfect fit). Reported here on data the model never saw during training. |
| **RMSE / MAE** | Average size of the model's prediction error, in rupees. RMSE penalizes big misses more; MAE treats all misses equally. |
| **MAPE** | Average prediction error as a percentage of actual revenue — easier to compare across time periods of different sizes. |
| **Budget optimization** | Using the fitted model to find the split of a fixed budget across channels that is predicted to generate the most revenue. |
| **What-if scenario** | A hypothetical budget or channel mix, run through the same fitted model, to see the predicted outcome before actually spending the money. |
